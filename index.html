Why the norm calculator exists

Chess-Results publishes a performance rating on every player card. It is computed under the FIDE Rating Regulations, which cap any rating difference above 400 points and know nothing about titles. Norms are governed by the Title Regulations instead, which have no 400-point rule and do have an adjusted rating floor that depends on which title you are chasing.

The two arithmetics disagree, and the difference is routinely large enough to decide whether you have a norm. The same games give four different performance ratings depending on the title, and the number on your player card is none of them.

The mistake almost every calculator makes

Article 1.4.6c permits one opponent to be raised to the adjusted rating floor, and it must be the lowest rated one. Raising every opponent below the floor inflates your average, sometimes by tens of points. This tool raises exactly one, shows you which, and shows you by how much.

What these tools will not tell you

A performance rating is not a norm. A norm additionally requires the right mix of opponent titles and federations, a minimum number of games, and a licensed arbiter's certificate. Nothing here checks those, and nothing here will congratulate you on a title you may not have. The final word belongs to your arbiter and to FIDE.

Calculations follow the FIDE Handbook: Title Regulations B.01 effective 1 January 2024 for norm performance, and the FIDE (Dutch) System C.04.3 for Round 1 pairing.
